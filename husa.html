Home Repair Plans (HomeServe-style) — the 5+1 Holds

A subscription home-repair-plan business: a vetted contractor network doing the actual repairs, 24/7 claims with dispatch, a one-year repair guarantee, and an HVAC install business on the side. The generality test: Supplier becomes the contractor network (the trades are the supply chain) and Operations becomes the claims-and-jobs engine. The utility-partner channel is real but deliberately held off the theater for now — Demand Creation manages that relationship by contract (partners realize it natively or behind an adapter, below the line), and the commercial paper is out-of-band.

What the second example proves

The 5+1 held without strain — only the lexicons changed. Supplier's supply chain is people (the trades), not protein bars, and Operations is a claims-and-jobs engine. Note the terminal choice: Enroll-to-Covered ends at CoverageEffective (a clock-driven edge — the waiting period), not PlanSold. Same lesson as FirstClassAttended: the stream ends when the value is real, not when the paperwork is.

What deliberately isn't on this grid

The utility-partner channel — real, and half the funnel — is held off the theater for now: Demand Creation manages the relationship as a contracted capability (partners implement the contract natively or sit behind an ACL adapter, both below the line), revenue share and the master agreement are out-of-band treaty paper, and a Partner-to-Program stream earns its seat only if partner programs become a managed population. Contractor-to-Network (ContractorApplied → ContractorActivated · ∅ ApplicationRejected · CredentialLapsed) is a real stream — but it crosses only Supplier + Enabling, so it appears as a column on the Supplier grid, one level down. Same rule that put the channels on the Homeowner grid: a composition surfaces at the level where it crosses boundaries. And the Operations check: repair claims and HVAC installs are two work-types, but they share the other five spheres — so one Operations sphere at theater, with a claims-ops / install-ops split as the first candidate decomposition on its grid.

Streams chain — here too

PlanCancelled opens Win-back (an unhappy terminal is another stream's trigger); RepairVerifiedClosed triggers Contractor Settlement's money movement; a repair visit can emit InstallLeadCaptured, feeding Quote-to-Install — the repair business is the install business's best marketer, and the grid shows that as a fact crossing a seam, not as a dotted line on a slide. JobUnfulfilled — no contractor capacity in territory — is the ugliest terminal and the most important one to declare: it's the fact the Network Capacity capability exists to prevent, and the one a leader most needs on a dashboard.

The analytical column behaves

Loss ratio is a reshape — premiums (Finance words) over claims costs (Finance words) — so it lives in the Insights column as a product, referencing the Claims Cost Ledger. Churn risk and contractor scoring mint new words, so they are capabilities with homes (Homeowner Experience and Supplier, respectively) that the column merely uses. Mints-vs-reshapes, applied twice, no exceptions needed.
