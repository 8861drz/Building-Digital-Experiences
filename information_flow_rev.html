PART ONE

The Business Story

Written for gym owners and business stakeholders.

1. The Organisation on OpenGym

A gym on OpenGym is not a single system. It is a collection of business domains — each responsible for a distinct part of how the gym operates, each with its own information and its own decisions to make.

These domains share information — but only through well-defined handoff points. When something happens in one part of the business, the other parts learn about it and react. The mechanism that makes this work is the business event — a formal record of a fact that has occurred.

 

Domain

Reacts To

Business Consequence

Sales & Marketing

New order, lead, campaign

The door through which new members enter. Owns the offer and closes the sale.

Customer

Member relationship, balance, arrears

The ongoing member relationship after joining. Knows every member by name, payment status, and history.

Operations

Schedule, bookings, attendance

Delivery of the service the member paid for. Classes, coaches, entitlements, attendance records.

Finance

Ledger, cash, revenue, reporting

The financial shadow of the business. Records the accounting consequence of every business event. Never manages member relationships.

Supplier

Partners, retail inventory, referrals

External relationships — partner gyms, suppliers, referral arrangements.

 

The key principle: Each domain minds its own business. Finance does not manage member relationships. Customer does not process cash. Operations does not recognise revenue. Each domain does its own job and learns what it needs to know from events other domains publish.

 

2. What Is a Business Event?

A business event is a formal record that something has happened. Not a request — a fact. When a member places an order, OrderCreated is published. Every domain that needs to know receives a copy and reacts independently. No domain waited for another. No domain called another.

This is what makes the system resilient — and what makes it possible to add new capabilities later without rebuilding what already works. A loyalty programme added next year subscribes to events that already exist. The ordering system does not change.

 

Why this matters for your gym: If you add a loyalty programme, a new product type, or a partner network, those capabilities subscribe to existing events. The existing business keeps running. New capabilities plug in.

 

3. The Member Journey

Follow a single member — Jane — from signup through her first month of active membership.

3.1 Jane Places an Order

Jane signs up online for a monthly membership at $99/month. She clicks confirm. One thing happens at the platform level: OrderCreated is published. From that single event, five different things happen simultaneously across the business.

 

Domain

Reacts To

Business Consequence

Sales & Marketing

OrderCreated

Closes the lead. Updates conversion reporting. Stops acquisition campaigns for Jane. Records channel attribution.

Customer

OrderCreated

Creates Jane's member record. Sends welcome email. Sets up her balance ($99 due). Begins payment collection. Schedules dunning if needed.

Operations

OrderCreated

Creates Jane's entitlement — her right to book classes. Status: pending payment confirmation.

Finance

OrderCreated

Records accounting entry: Accounts Receivable debited, Revenue credited (monthly) or Deferred Revenue (quarterly/annual). No member name stored.

Supplier

OrderCreated

If the order came via a partner channel: records referral obligation. Otherwise uninvolved.

 

3.2 Jane's Payment Clears

Jane's card is charged. The payment processor confirms settlement. PaymentCleared is published by the Finance payment component. Two domains react:

Customer clears Jane's balance to zero. Any payment reminder sequences stop. Entitlement is confirmed active. Payment confirmation sent to Jane.

Finance closes the Accounts Receivable entry. DR Cash / CR Accounts Receivable. The obligation is settled for this period.

Neither domain called the other. Both learned from PaymentCleared. Same fact, two reactions, no coordination required.

 

3.3 Jane Attends Her First Class

Jane books through the member app and attends. The coach marks her present. AttendanceRecorded is published. For a monthly subscription paid and recognised already, this is primarily an operational signal — the service was delivered. For Customer it is a retention signal: members who attend in week one are significantly more likely to still be members in month three.

 

3.4 End of Month — Renewal

Jane's first month closes. For a monthly subscription, revenue was recognised when payment cleared — no deferred balance to amortise. The month-end entry is already on the books. SubscriptionRenewalDue is published. Customer initiates the charge. The cycle repeats.

 

4. Retail Purchases and the Tab

Most gyms sell more than memberships. Supplements, drinks, merchandise. Charging each $4 item through a card payment individually would cost the gym significant processing fees.

The platform uses a tab model. Retail purchases during a visit accumulate on the member's tab. The tab is settled as a single charge at visit end — one transaction, one fee.

 

Domain

Reacts To

Business Consequence

Member buys a drink

RetailItemAdded

Customer adds to open tab. Finance records DR Accounts Receivable / CR Sales. No card charge yet.

Member buys another item

RetailItemAdded

Added to same tab. Balance grows.

Visit ends

TabClosed

Customer triggers a single charge for the full tab amount.

Payment clears

PaymentCleared

Customer clears tab balance. Finance closes AR, records cash.

 

The tab is Customer's concept entirely. Finance sees an AR entry when the tab opens and a cash entry when it clears. Retail line items never appear on the general ledger individually.

 

PART TWO

Information Flow in Detail

Written for developers and solution architects.

5. OrderCreated — The Founding Event

Every member relationship begins with OrderCreated. It is general enough to represent any offering — membership, class pack, retail bundle, partner product — without prescribing downstream behaviour. The offeringType and billingCycle fields are the routing keys each consumer uses to decide what to do.

 

OrderCreated

orderId

Stable identifier. Every subsequent event in this member's lifecycle references this.

memberId

Platform identity of the purchaser.

tenantId

Which gym. Extracted from the member's Trust Envelope — never a parameter.

channel

DIRECT | PARTNER | ONLINE | IN_PERSON — where the order originated.

orderLines[]

One or more line items. Each carries offeringId, offeringType, quantity, unitPrice.

offeringType

MEMBERSHIP | CLASS_PACK | DROP_IN | RETAIL — drives domain behaviour.

totalAmount

Sum of all line items. Minor currency units (cents).

currency

ISO 4217. e.g. USD.

paymentTerms

IMMEDIATE | SUBSCRIPTION_RECURRING | TAB

billingCycle

MONTHLY | QUARTERLY | ANNUAL. Present for SUBSCRIPTION_RECURRING only.

 

Design principle: OrderCreated is intentionally broad. A new product type is added by defining a new offeringType value. Each domain decides independently how to handle it. The publisher does not need to know what its consumers will do.

 

6. Finance and Customer — Two Views of the Same Money

The most important boundary in the revenue flow is between Finance and Customer. They handle complementary concerns and must never be conflated.

 

Domain

Reacts To

Business Consequence

Finance

Ledger-level only

Journal entries, account codes, revenue recognition, cash, financial reporting. Never stores a member name. Never manages a member relationship.

Customer

Member-level only

Member balances, payment instruments, arrears, dunning sequences, refunds. Never posts a journal entry. Never processes real cash.

 

Cash arrives at the business through Finance's payment processing component — not through Customer. When a payment settles, PaymentCleared is published. Finance records the cash. Customer clears the member balance. Both learned from the same event. Neither called the other.

Customer knows Jane owes $99. Finance knows account code 1200 (AR) has a $99 debit. These are the same commercial fact expressed in two completely different languages. The event is what keeps them consistent.

A member cancels their annual subscription after four months. Customer processes the request and publishes SubscriptionCancelled.

When processed.

When access ends. May differ from cancelledAt — e.g. end of paid period.

Amount to refund. Zero if cancelling at end of paid period.

Remaining deferred balance reversed. Refund issued for the unearned portion. Recognised revenue (months 1–4) requires no reversal.

 

10.2 Operations reacts

•        EntitlementSuspended published for this member.

•        Any future class bookings after effectiveDate are cancelled.

•        ReservationCancelled published for each affected booking.

 

10.3 Customer reacts

•        Member record updated: status = CANCELLED.

•        Recurring billing sequence stopped.

•        Cancellation confirmation sent to member.

•        Retention sequence triggered if gym policy permits a save attempt.

 

Three domains. One event. Each acted independently and in parallel. The refund, the entitlement suspension, and the member communication are not coordinated — they are consequences of the same published fact.

11. Payment Failure and Arrears

A subscription renewal fails — card declined. PaymentFailed is published by Finance's payment component.

 

Domain

Reacts To

Business Consequence

Customer

PaymentFailed

Opens arrears record. Begins dunning: day 1 reminder, day 3 follow-up, day 7 final notice. Manages all member communication and retry logic.

Operations

PaymentFailed

Grace period: entitlement stays active. After grace period expires with no payment: EntitlementSuspended published.

Finance

PaymentFailed

AR entry stays open. No cash. No revenue reversal — revenue was recognised, the obligation to pay remains. Finance monitors AR ageing.

 

If the member pays during the dunning window, PaymentCleared is published and all three domains react exactly as in Section 8. If payment never arrives, MemberInArrears is published by Customer. Operations suspends the entitlement. Finance provisions the AR balance as doubtful debt if gym policy requires.

 

PART THREE

Why This Model Stays Flexible

Written for both audiences.

12. Adding Capabilities Without Rebuilding

The flows in this document describe a monthly membership. The same event structure handles class packs, drop-ins, retail tabs, partner network passes, and any future product the business creates.

This is the result of one design decision: the event is the only shared language between domains. Each domain subscribes to the events it cares about and ignores the rest. New capabilities plug in by subscribing to existing events. The existing business does not change.

Adding a Loyalty Programme

•        AttendanceRecorded already exists. The loyalty programme subscribes to it and awards points. No change to Operations.

•        RetailItemAdded already exists. The loyalty programme subscribes for redemption logic. No change to Customer or the tab system.

•        The loyalty programme is a new capability. It plugs into existing events. Zero changes to the existing platform.

 

Switching a Scheduling Vendor

•        The scheduling vendor's job is to publish ClassPublished and ClassCancelled.

•        An adapter translates the new vendor's native format into the OpenGym event contract.

•        Reservation, Operations, Finance — no changes. They subscribe to the contract, not the vendor.

 

Adding a Partner Gym

•        ParticipationAgreementActivated is published. Reservation subscribes and begins routing cross-tenant bookings.

•        Partner classes appear in the member app. Revenue settlement is tracked by Platform Billing.

•        No changes to Scheduling, Finance, or Customer.

 

The event is stable. The reactions are composable. A new capability that needs to react to something that already happens in the business simply subscribes to the existing event. This is why the event contract is treated as a first-class design artifact — not documentation written after the fact.

Every domain reacts to the facts it cares about. The ledger reflects the business. The member relationship is managed where it belongs. Revenue is recognised when it is earned.
